Snack Guild — Product Document (MVP PRD)

Team 33 · Version 0.1 · Consolidated from Missions 2, 3 and 4

1. Product Overview

One-line positioning: An AI matching platform that connects academic research with industry decision-makers (corporate R&D, investors, technology scouts). A company describes a need, the system returns a shortlist of researchers with evidence and explanations, and an introduction request replaces cold outreach.

Vision: Turn scattered academic output into industry impact, funding and partnerships.

MVP goal: Validate one core hypothesis: Does AI matching help companies find relevant researchers more effectively than keyword search, and will users actually request introductions?

2. Problem and Opportunity

Current state Consequence
Researcher expertise is scattered across papers, lab pages, LinkedIn, conferences and personal networks Companies spend significant time finding experts and depend on existing relationships
Researchers have no time to promote their work (a PI may juggle ~25 projects) Results never reach industry; funding, collaboration and commercialization opportunities are missed
Cold outreach gets ~5% response rates Neither side can cheaply find the right counterpart

Market gap: Existing tools (paper indexes, LinkedIn, general social media) each cover one piece. None is purpose-built for matching research with the people who fund, license, hire or commercialize it, and none offers IP-aware trust infrastructure.

Note: Problem size, pain frequency and willingness to pay are hypotheses to be validated through interviews, not established facts.

3. Target Users

Demand side (paying customers)

  • Persona: Daniel Chen, 38. Principal at an early-stage deep-tech VC fund who also advises corporate R&D teams on technology scouting. Looks for projects to invest in and technologies to license.
  • Others: corporate R&D and innovation teams, recruiters seeking research talent.

Supply side

  • Persona: Dr. Sarah Miller, 41. Associate Professor leading a 12-person lab; wants her research to reach industry, funding and collaborators.
  • Others: postdocs, PhD students, labs, universities.

Pain point priorities

Priority Pain point Product implication
Core 1. Fragmented discovery 2. No reliable path to the right counterpart Primary value: topic-based discovery plus a direct path to the right person
Adoption barrier 3. Researchers have no time to communicate Decides cold-start success; posting effort must be minimal
Trust conditions 4. Hard to judge credibility/readiness 5. Fear of sharing too early Design constraints: evidence links and visibility control

4. Core User Flow (MVP Main Path)

Company side

  1. Create an organization profile and choose the type of opportunity (research collaboration, sponsored research, consulting, talent recruitment, licensing)
  2. Enter a natural-language need, e.g. "We are looking for researchers working on computer vision and anomaly detection for industrial quality inspection."
  3. View an AI-generated ranked shortlist with match explanation and evidence
  4. Click Request Introduction (optionally using an AI-generated outreach draft, which must be reviewed and approved by the user before sending)

Researcher side

  1. Create or view a profile: research topics, publications, projects, methods, skills, (optional) patents
  2. Set which opportunity types they are open to
  3. Receive an introduction request, then accept or decline; if accepted, both parties proceed to contact

Fallback states

  • Insufficient information: the system asks a clarifying question, e.g. "Are you primarily looking for research collaboration, hiring, licensing, or consulting?"
  • No strong match: the system says so explicitly rather than presenting weak matches as highly relevant

6. AI Design

Input: Company natural-language need plus opportunity type Data: Researcher topics, publication titles/abstracts, projects, methods, skills, patents (where available)

Suggested lightweight implementation:

  1. Request parsing: An LLM extracts technical concepts, application area and desired engagement type
  2. Retrieval: Embedding similarity search over researcher profiles to recall the top N, optionally combined with keyword filters
  3. Re-ranking and explanation: An LLM judges relevance for each candidate and writes an explanation that cites only evidence present in the profile
  4. Confidence threshold: Matches below the threshold are not shown; if all fall below it, return "no strong match"

Guardrails:

  • Never claim a researcher is definitively the best fit or that a collaboration will succeed
  • Every explanation must trace back to a specific publication or project in the profile (to limit hallucination)
  • No automated claims of commercial viability and no investment recommendations
  • No autonomous outreach: every message requires human approval

7. Minimal Data Model

  • Researcher: id, name, institution, verified (bool), topics[], methods[], skills[], open_to[], visibility
  • Publication: id, researcher_id, title, abstract, year, link
  • Project: id, researcher_id, title, description
  • Organization: id, name, type (company/VC/recruiter), description
  • Request: id, org_id, text, opportunity_type, extracted_concepts[]
  • Match: id, request_id, researcher_id, score, explanation, evidence_refs[]
  • Introduction: id, match_id, draft_message, status (pending/accepted/declined), timestamps

Initial data: A small, manually curated dataset (roughly 20 to 50 researchers) using permitted public information or clearly labeled demonstration profiles.

8. Evaluation Plan

Questions to test (from Mission 4; report actual observed results rather than claiming success in advance):

  1. Does a request produce relevant matches?
  2. Are explanations grounded in the researcher's information?
  3. Can a user understand why a researcher was selected?
  4. Can the user successfully request an introduction?
  5. Does the workflow communicate the next step clearly?

AI vs non-AI baseline: Run 5 to 10 test requests through both keyword/filter search and AI matching. Have 3 to 5 target users rate relevance blind. Compare top-3 hit rate, time to a usable candidate, and explanation trust ratings.

9. Business Model (Hypotheses to Validate)

  • Primary revenue: Tiered B2B subscription for companies, corporate R&D, recruiters and investors (advanced discovery, matching, monitoring)
  • Secondary: Qualified introduction/lead fees; sponsored challenges and research opportunity postings; institutional subscriptions for universities and research organizations
  • Top three revenue hypotheses:
    1. Companies will pay if the product saves meaningful time versus manual searching
    2. Companies and recruiters will pay to discover PhDs, postdocs and researchers by research expertise rather than job boards and resumes
    3. Universities and research organizations will value increased external visibility and measurable connections with companies

The MVP includes no payment processing; willingness to pay is tested through interviews or a landing page.

10. Scope Boundaries

Human-supported (manual / Wizard-of-Oz in MVP): Verifying researcher identity and institutional affiliation, moderating profiles, reviewing questionable matches, facilitating the first introduction, helping organizations create their first opportunity, curating the initial dataset, handling sensitive/IP questions, following up after introductions.

Deliberately out of scope: Investment transactions, payment processing, grant administration, legal contracts, IP agreements, university ERP integrations, full ATS functionality, full academic social networking, full-scale paper indexing, autonomous outreach without human approval, automated claims of commercial viability, automated investment recommendations, ingestion of confidential/unpublished research.

11. Trust and Privacy

  • Evidence links to public papers, projects, patents and institutional profiles
  • Researchers control: what is publicly displayed, which opportunities they are open to, whether they respond, and what confidential research they choose not to disclose
  • Companies control: the problem they submit, which researchers they view, whether they make contact, and whether a collaboration proceeds
  • The platform does not ingest any unpublished or confidential research
